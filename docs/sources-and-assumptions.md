# Sources and assumptions

Every number the engine uses is one of five kinds, and this register lists them all. The code keeps the full citation next to each constant (resource ids included); this is the index. **Published** and **standard** values come from someone else. **Derived** values are computed from published data by a stated method. **Assumptions** and **heuristics** are our choices: each is listed with why, and none is tuned to make results look better. Change one only with a source, and update this table in the same change.

## Planning assumptions (chosen, with the reason)

| What | Value | Why | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tap-changer target voltage** | **1.02 pu** | LTDS publishes no AVC targets, and GB DNOs set them above nominal so feeder ends stay in band at peak. 1.02 is the lowest value at which today's network meets the statutory band at winter peak (at 1.00 Horncastle 33 kV sits at 0.932 pu). Chosen, not sourced. | `config.OLTC_SETPOINT_PU` |
| Tap-changer deadband | ±0.01 pu | A typical AVC relay deadband | `config.OLTC_DEADBAND_PU` |
| Tap step | 1.25% per tap | LTDS gives each tap range but not the step; a typical GB value | `config.OLTC_TAP_STEP_PERCENT` |
| **Voltage-collapse floor** | **0.5 pu** | A converged power flow with any in-service busbar below this, after the tap changers have settled, is the low-voltage solution of the equations, not an operating point. The case is reported as **no valid operating point (voltage collapse)**, naming the lowest busbar and its voltage, the way a case that does not converge is reported; no loading or breach from it is reported. In a measured or grown year such a half-hour counts as not solved, and the count of collapses is kept apart from non-convergence. *Why 0.5:* for a constant-power load fed through a reactance, the highest voltage at which the lower, unstable branch of the solution begins (the nose of its P–V curve) is V = E / √(2(1 + sin φ)): 0.71 pu at unity power factor, 0.56 at 0.8 lagging (E = 1.0). So below 0.5 a solution is on the lower branch for any demand at 0.8 lagging or better, which covers every LTDS power factor. *Context:* the lowest busbar voltage in any Bicker Fen plan year, measured year or grown year on file is far above it (stated in the decisions file, item 52). *What it is not:* not a statutory limit (the statutory bands, `VOLTAGE_BANDS`, are unchanged, and a busbar between the floor and its band is still reported as a voltage breach), and not a stability study (a lower-branch solution above 0.5 pu, possible for loads near unity power factor, is not detected) | `config.COLLAPSE_VM_FLOOR_PU`, `network._solve_with` |
| Generation at winter peak | PV and wind 0; batteries and hybrids charging at full import; gas, biomass and unclassified firm | The SQSS security condition excludes intermittent generation at peak; EREP 130's F factors would credit a little, the engine credits none. ## The measured year

A year of NGED's half-hourly flows run through the same AC power flow, to count the hours each line and transformer
spends above its rating (`nged_flows.py` maps NGED's files onto the network, `timeseries.py` runs the year). The basis
and the check are in `docs/reports/timeseries-step1-spec-2026-11-12.md`.

| What | Kind | Value and reason |
|---|---|---|

Bus names come from NGED's *Bus Mapping* (`6ba4ccd6-a9f8-405e-a3cf-45c79de9f16e`). Node flows are metered net flows (demand positive), cleansed and gap-filled by NGED with no flag; branch flows are NGED's own load flow on them. Fetched to a local, git-ignored path, not committed (repository size). Supported by NGED Open Data |

| Reactive-power screen | Heuristic | A node's half-hour is **implausible** when its apparent power exceeds 1.5 × the installed transformer capacity at its substation (the sum of its LTDS transformer ratings), and **suspect** when its MVAr exceeds twice its MW and 2 MVAr. A node whose suspect flags exceed 1% of the year with no implausible half-hour is listed, not substituted. Added after the year's results, for a demonstrated fault in NGED's published MVAr: Spilsby reads up to 54 MVAr through 2 × 6 MVA transformers, and Billingborough 34 MVAr in one half-hour. The run on the published MVAr is kept and reported beside it. |
| The year view's labels | Derived | Two things judged apart. **Agreement with NGED's model** is a property of the network: each line and transformer is judged by the measured year's rules on the year that closes best, **2025 screened** (rule 1 on 94% of half-hours, against 60% for 2024). Every element NGED's 2024 file compares, its 2025 file compares too (164 elements against 158: its newer model edition adds six); each label carries the year it was judged on (`label_year`). **Input reliability** is a property of the flows a run is built from: a node whose reactive input the screen flags on more than 1% of the year makes its transformers "not a finding", its busbars not findings and its substation absent from the firm-capacity list, judged on the measured run of the run's own source flows (2024's for the 2024 measured runs and every grown or branch year built on them, 2025's for the 2025 run and the grown year on 2025's flows). ## Comparison with NGED's half-hourly flows

NGED publishes half-hourly flows for the Bicker Fen group for 2024 (its curtailment-analysis datasets). They come in two kinds:
- **Per substation node:** metered net flow, which NGED has cleansed and gap-filled with imputed values. The imputed values carry no flag.
- **Per branch:** flows from NGED's own load flow (PSS/E), run on those node values every half-hour.

The engine beside them, both ways: NGED's 2024 half-hours run through it (the measured year, run B of
`docs/reports/measured-year-2024/report.md`), and today's two planning cases:

| Quantity | NGED, 2024 | Engine, 2024 measured year (run B) | Engine, today's planning cases |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net flow at the Bicker Fen 132 kV busbar | 278 MW export max, 49 MW import max (NGED's GSP node series); 209 and 132 MW through NGED's four circuits into it | 216 MW export max, 137 MW import max | 340 MW export at summer minimum, 144 MW import at winter peak (292 and 137 before the named generators were placed, Ash Farm added and Warth Lane restored) |
| Bicker Fen to Boston circuit, flow towards the GSP (NGED's load flow) | 85 MW max | 86 MW max | 102 MW at summer minimum |
| Bicker Fen to Boston circuit, flow towards Boston (NGED's load flow) | 27 MW max | 30 MW max | 27 MW at winter peak |

This is a check of scale, not a match to observation:
- **The GSP node series is unexplained.** It does not reconcile with the four 132 kV circuits that NGED's own load flow carries into Bicker Fen. On 95% of half-hours it shows more export than they do, by a median of 52 MW, and nothing in the published files accounts for the gap. Its figures are quoted as NGED publishes them, not as a measurement of the busbar.
- **The planning cases are extremes by design.** Summer minimum has every generator at full output at the minimum demand; winter peak credits no wind (the SQSS security condition). So they sit above NGED's figures.
